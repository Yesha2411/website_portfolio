The Scale: This wasn't a standalone feature. It was a module inside Nucleus — LEAD's ERP system used by 3,500+ schools across India, managed by 9 Product Managers. I was the designer for the principal and Area Manager side of the product.

02 — Context

The Scale Behind the Brief

This wasn't a startup feature. This was infrastructure used daily by thousands of people across India.

The Platform Nucleus — LEAD School's ERP system. Academic management, performance monitoring, onboarding, reporting.
The Scale 3,500+ schools across India. 9 Product Managers owning different modules within the same system.
My Users Principals managing their schools. Area Managers (AMs) visiting multiple schools per week to observe, coach, and report.
My Position Solo designer on the principal and AM side. Embedded with the PM leading this project. Already knew the users.

Working across 9 PMs on a single ERP system meant design decisions had real downstream consequences. A pattern I introduced in one module could affect how another PM's team designed theirs. Speed mattered — but so did coherence. Every decision had to work inside a system being used at national scale.

"I didn't design a feature. I designed a module inside an operating system for thousands of schools."

03 — Problem

A Full Day. A Google Doc. A Burnout.

Area Managers were visiting schools to observe teachers, coach principals, and document everything. Then going home and spending hours rebuilding those observations in a Google Doc.

The existing OAC process was entirely manual. AMs visited schools, took notes on paper or in personal docs, then reconstructed a formal report from memory using PDFs and Google Docs — often after a full day of school visits. That report could take 3–4 hours to complete.

For AMs visiting multiple schools per week, this wasn't inconvenient — it was a burnout machine. High turnover. Low-quality reports. Late submissions. Schools waiting on feedback that arrived days after the visit.

The problem wasn't that AMs lacked information. They had everything — they'd just lived it that day. The problem was the tool-to-reality gap: no system that matched how they actually worked in the field.

Goals for the solution:

→ Reduce report creation time by up to 80%
→ Mobile-first — reports completed from the field, not the desk
→ Inclusive and accessible design for varying digital literacy
→ Auto-selection logic to reduce cognitive load in the field

04 — Research Advantage

Speed Earned by Depth

I could move fast because I'd already done the slow work. The 48-hour POC wasn't improvised — it was accumulated.

I was already embedded in the same product team as the PM leading this project. That meant I came into the OAC brief with context most designers would have needed weeks to build:

40+ user interviews with principals — understanding how school leaders experienced the post-visit feedback loop. What they wanted to know. How they processed the information. Where the current system broke their workflow.

2 focus group sessions with Area Managers — specifically on how they conducted school visits. Their note-taking habits. Their mental model for observation categories. Their frustration with the reporting lag between visit and submission.

The main findings that shaped every design decision:

→ AMs needed to capture observations while still in the school — not reconstruct them from memory hours later
→ The report structure had to mirror how an AM thinks during a visit: objectives first, then teacher-by-teacher observations, then overall feedback
→ Auto-selection of teacher names and class details was critical — typing in the field is friction that kills adoption
→ Draft state management was essential — visits get interrupted; the app had to survive real-world conditions

05 — The POC

48 Hours. Zero Guessing.

The stakeholder meeting was in 48 hours. The PM needed a validated proof of concept. They came to me.

Not because I was the only designer available. Because I was the designer who already knew this product, these users, and this problem space. The embedded knowledge I'd built from months of research and PM collaboration meant I could move at a speed that would have looked reckless from the outside — but was actually precise.

In 48 hours I delivered:

→ Low-fidelity wireframes mapping the complete mobile-first user flow — report creation, teacher observation entry, overall school feedback, picworthy moment capture
→ Progressive disclosure architecture — the form revealed itself as the AM worked through the visit, never overwhelming them with the full complexity at once
→ Auto-selection logic — teacher names, class details, and school information pre-populated from the Nucleus backend
→ Draft state management — partial reports saved automatically, resumable from any device
→ New design system components aligned with LEAD's marketing guidelines — because this wasn't just a new feature, it was a new surface that needed to feel coherent with the broader product

The wireframes went through rapid iteration — from paper-level sketches to structured mobile frames to a stakeholder-ready POC. The flow covered every state: empty report, in-progress observation, auto-selection modal, draft list, published report view.

06 — Delivery

What We Shipped

From POC to production — 50+ screens across mobile, desktop, and tablet. New design system. Live in thousands of schools.

After the POC was greenlit, the project moved into full hi-fidelity design. The mobile-first approach was preserved and extended to responsive desktop and tablet views — because while AMs completed reports on mobile in the field, principals reviewed them on desktop, and the experience had to be seamless across all surfaces.

Mobile screens: Report creation flow, teacher feedback sections, engagement rating inputs, classroom behaviour management, picworthy moment capture with photo upload, draft save and resume, published report list.

Desktop screens: Principal review view, school-level performance summary, comparative report history, export and share flows.

New design system components: Built to LEAD's marketing guidelines — a visual language update that brought the OAC module into alignment with the brand's newer direction while remaining consistent with the existing Nucleus system.

The full module was completed in approximately one month from POC to production handoff. My foundation — the POC, the component library, the interaction model — was what the final build was built on.

07 — Impact & Reflection

The Numbers. The Reflection.

A full-day process became a 10-minute one. That's not a UX win — that's an operational transformation.

96%Task time reduction
10 minReport completion time
+80%User efficiency increase

Area Managers who previously spent 3–4 hours reconstructing a school visit from memory were completing the same report in 10 minutes — during the visit itself. The quality of observations improved because they were captured in context, not reconstructed from fading memory. Submission rates increased. Principals received feedback faster.

The Best Group Award for this project wasn't just recognition for the POC — it was recognition for the entire team's ability to move from a validated concept to a live product without losing the integrity of the original design thinking.

What I'd do differently: I'd have pushed for a usability test with AMs in the field before the first production release — not just the focus groups during research. The real test of a mobile-first field tool is when someone is standing in a classroom with bad lighting and a time constraint. That's the environment I would have tested in from the beginning.

What this project proves: That speed and depth aren't opposites. The fastest work I've done has always been grounded in the most thorough research. Knowing your users well enough to make confident decisions quickly — that's not luck. That's a system.

"I don't ship fast because I skip steps — I ship fast because I think in systems. The OAC Report was 48 hours of output backed by months of understanding."
